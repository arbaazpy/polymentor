BEHAVIORAL INTERVIEW EXPERT — QUICK REFERENCE CARD
==================================================

Version 0.1.0. Lane: behav. Public knowledge synthesis only. This card repeats
the critical lists and phrases from the main corpus.

CORE PHRASES TO ECHO
--------------------
- STAR = Situation, Task, Action, Result; Action and Result carry the signal.
- A good behavioral story is specific, first-person, and quantified where
  possible.
- Red flag: the candidate blames the team. Green flag: the candidate owns the
  outcome and names the lesson.
- Past behavior predicts future behavior, so behavioral questions beat
  hypothetical ones as evidence.
- Keep Situation and Task to about two sentences; spend the time on Action and
  Result.

THE EIGHT SITUATIONAL BUCKETS
-----------------------------
1. Conflict: a disagreement with a teammate or manager.
2. Failure: something you owned that went wrong.
3. Leadership: leading a project or a team.
4. Ambiguity: unclear requirements.
5. Influence without authority: convincing people who don't report to you.
6. Prioritization: too much to do.
7. Feedback: feedback received, or difficult feedback given.
8. Ownership: going beyond your assigned scope.

THE FOUR-CRITERION STORY RUBRIC
-------------------------------
1. Specificity: one concrete event with real details.
2. Ownership: first-person actions; owns the outcome, including mistakes.
3. Quantification: a number or a clear before-and-after.
4. Lesson: what you learned and what you now do differently.
Score each 0, 1 or 2. Strong story 7 to 8, mid story 4 to 6, weak 3 or below.

PROBING PATTERNS
----------------
- "What did you do?" separates personal action from team action.
- "What would you do differently?" tests the lesson.
- "How did you measure that?" tests quantification.
- "What did the other person think?" tests empathy in conflict.
- "What happened next?" tests whether the result lasted.

COMMON FAILURES AND FIXES
-------------------------
- Vague story: habits instead of one event; pick a single incident.
- "We" only: unclear personal action; probe with "What did you do?" and
  rewrite the Action in the first person.
- No result: add a measured outcome.
- Blaming others: red flag; own your part and name the lesson.
- Over-rehearsed: know the facts, not a script.
- Too much setup: cut context to two sentences.
- Fake failure ("I work too hard"): choose a real mistake.

STORY BANK
----------
Prepare six to ten real stories before interviews. Tag each story with the
buckets it can answer, so one strong story can serve several questions; a
migration you led might cover leadership, ambiguity and prioritization. Check
every story against the rubric: specificity, ownership, quantification,
lesson. Rehearse the facts, not a script, so the story survives probing.

WORKED GRADING EXAMPLE
----------------------
"We had a bug and we fixed it and everyone was happy." Specificity 0,
ownership 0, quantification 0, lesson 0: a weak story. Probe with "What did
you do?" and "How did you measure that?"

ABSTENTION / DELEGATION
-----------------------
- System design questions: delegate to sysdes.
- Algorithms and complexity: delegate to dsa.
- Resume bullets and job-description fit: delegate to resume.
- Not covered: salary negotiation, offer evaluation, visa questions. Say it is
  not covered.
- Never invent a story for the candidate; coach structure, ask for real
  details.

CHECKLIST
---------
STAR with the weight on Action and Result. Specific, first-person, quantified.
Rubric: specificity, ownership, quantification, lesson. A story bank across
the eight buckets. Own failures and name the lesson.
